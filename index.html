You don't need to be good at dancing. You need to be curious about the person in front of you, and willing to be surprised by the music.

The approach

Three things we practise, every single lesson.

01

Connection

Weight, breath and intention, not memorised steps. You'll learn to send clear invitations, receive honest answers and stay with a partner through the in-between moments.

02

Musicality

Hear the layers: bass, melody, silence. We train your ear so the music tells you what to do next, because fusion means dancing blues, tango, zouk, contact and everything between to whatever song comes on.

03

Play

Mistakes are material. Games, constraints and role swaps keep the lesson light, so you stop performing and start discovering what your body already knows.

The foundations

Four ideas that hold every dance.

Whatever the song or the style, fusion rests on the same four building blocks. Every private lesson trains them, in whatever order serves you best.

01

Frame

Your posture, balance and the shape you hold with a partner. A good frame is relaxed but alive: it carries clear signals without gripping, and keeps you steady when everything else is moving.

  • Posture & alignment
  • Arms, hold & embrace
  • Grounding & balance
02

Turns

Pivots, spirals and rotations, solo and together. Clean turns come from a stable axis and a clear intention, so you can spin with ease and never lose your partner or your balance.

  • Axis & spotting
  • Pivots, spirals & wraps
  • Turning together
03

Steps

Walking, weight changes and footwork: the vocabulary of travel. We keep it simple and precise, because a confident walk is the most beautiful move in the room.

  • Walking & weight transfer
  • Footwork & directions
  • Patterns that stay flexible
04

Flow

Where it all comes together: linking moves without stopping, riding the music, and improvising from one moment to the next. Flow is what makes a dance feel like a conversation.

  • Transitions & momentum
  • Musical phrasing
  • Improvising together

Good to know

Questions, answered.

Do I need a partner or any experience?

Neither. Solo lessons are built for people on their own, and I'll partner with you throughout. Everything is taught from the ground up.

What should I wear?

Anything you can move in. Shoes that let you pivot (suede soles, socks or smooth trainers) work best. Skip heavy grippy soles.

How is a private lesson different from a group class?

Every minute is about you. I can see exactly what's happening in your dancing, adapt the pace instantly and focus on your goals, whether that's confidence, technique or a specific event.

Do you teach leading and following?

Yes, both, whenever you need them. You choose your role and can switch any time. Roles are about information flow, not gender, and learning both makes you a better dancer. In solo lessons I take the opposite role so you can feel it in real time.

How do you approach consent and comfort?

You can say no, take a break or change anything at any time, no explanation needed. Touch is always opt-in, and I'll check in often.

What's your cancellation policy?

Free rescheduling with 24 hours' notice. Life happens.
